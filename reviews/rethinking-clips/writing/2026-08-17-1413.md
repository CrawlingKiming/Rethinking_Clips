# ESS, batch size, and heuristic-threshold self-review

## Claim-evidence map

| Claim | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Effective support depends jointly on weight concentration and estimator size | Exact raw-gradient MSE identity and Corollary 9 | Supported |
| Batch size shifts the raw-versus-PPO MSE crossover | Fifty-six frozen states, eight per population-ESS interval, with 32 paired redraws at `N=128` and `N=512` | Supported in the controlled task |
| ESS locates the reliability regime after estimator size is fixed | PPO has lower mean MSE throughout at `N=128`; at `N=512`, the mean comparison changes sign between population ESS `[0.2,0.4)` and `[0.4,0.6)` | Supported in the controlled task |
| A universal normalized-ESS threshold follows from the theory | Neither the theorem nor the experiment supports this claim | Explicitly rejected |
| The `0.1` gate is useful in one fixed optimization configuration | One hundred paired runs; the gate reaches the doubling target with 25% fewer verifier responses than the permissive baseline and 40% fewer than PPO masking | Supported as a prespecified heuristic |
| The same `0.1` rule improves LLM RLVR across clipping variants | Main LLM experiments remain placeholders | Needs new experiment |

## End-of-paper self-review

### Contribution

1. **Pass.** The paper contributes the joint ESS and estimator-size view of gradient reliability.
2. **Pass.** It addresses the practically important failure of static clipping choices on repeatedly optimized rollout data.
3. **Pass.** The batch-size-dependent crossover is not implied by normalized ESS alone and follows from the clipping bias term surviving averaging.
4. **Pass in the controlled study.** The same ESS coordinate produces different estimator rankings at `N=128` and `N=512`.
5. **Pass.** The novelty is a theoretical bridge, a direct crossover diagnostic, and a separately evaluated heuristic gate.

### Writing clarity

1. **Pass.** The appendix specifies the task, exact oracle, seeds, checkpoint selection, redraw count, pairing, aggregation, and released files.
2. **Pass.** The estimator comparison and the optimization gate have separate protocols and claims.
3. **Pass.** Each component is motivated by the preceding theoretical implication.
4. **Pass.** Normalized ESS, effective sample count, sequence count, and independent block count are distinguished explicitly.
5. **Pass.** The narrative moves from support, to MSE, to policy improvement, to crossover, to the operational heuristic.

### Experimental strength

1. **Pass in scope.** The response-budget improvements are 25% and 40%, while the unresolved final gate-versus-PPO difference is stated honestly.
2. **Pass in scope.** The controlled study targets mechanism recovery rather than benchmark state of the art.
3. **Needs new experiment.** Cross-model and cross-task consistency awaits the matched LLM study.
4. **Pass.** The paper reports the absence of a crossover at `N=128` and does not hide uncertainty at `N=512`.

### Evaluation completeness

1. **Pass for the mechanism.** Estimator size, ESS regime, raw estimation, PPO masking, oracle updates, and the gate are separated.
2. **Needs new experiment.** PPO, Clip-Higher, and DPPO safeguards require matched LLM results.
3. **Pass.** Gradient MSE, exact reward change, response efficiency, final reward, uncertainty, and branch frequency are reported.
4. **Pass for internal validity; needs LLM evidence for external validity.** Optdigits permits exact population calculations but does not reproduce language generation.
5. **Pass.** The full statistical protocol and released CSV schema are documented.

### Method design soundness

1. **Pass in scope.** The simulation preserves RLVR rollout, grouped responses, sequential minibatches, recomputed ratios, and policy updates.
2. **Pass.** Within-prompt dependence is acknowledged, and the batch-size theorem is interpreted at the independent block level.
3. **Needs new experiment.** The `0.1` value is deliberately presented as configuration-specific until tested under LLM training.
4. **Pass in the controlled study.** The gate adds one ESS computation and masks only 5.17 of 128 updates on average.
5. **Pass in the controlled study.** The gate improves response efficiency without claiming a statistically resolved final-reward advantage over always masking.

## Reviewer-facing conclusion

The theory and controlled simulation now support a precise claim: ESS is a useful crossover coordinate only after estimator size and the training configuration are fixed.  The `0.1` value is not derived from the theorem.  It is a prespecified operational heuristic whose broader validity depends on the planned LLM experiments.
