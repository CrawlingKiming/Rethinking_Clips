# Policy-gradient convergence self-review

## Revised section outline

1. Define first-order stationarity as the standard finite-time target for a generally nonconcave policy objective.
2. Convert gradient-estimation error into one-step policy improvement through smoothness.
3. Telescope the one-step inequality into an explicit finite-time stationarity bound.
4. Separate exact convergence, convergence to a stationary neighborhood, iterate convergence, and global optimality.
5. Attribute the generic convergence argument to established policy-gradient and biased-gradient analyses.
6. State precisely when bounded parameters or projection are, and are not, required.
7. Substitute the ESS-dependent MSE bound to obtain the paper's estimator-specific convergence consequence.

1. **Contribution: pass in scope.** The revision no longer presents stationarity as an unexplained endpoint. It gives a finite-time convergence statement whose residual is exactly the gradient-MSE quantity studied by the paper.
2. **Writing clarity: pass.** The text distinguishes convergence of the gradient norm from convergence of parameters and from global optimality.
3. **Experimental strength: unchanged.** This revision changes only the theoretical interpretation and assumptions.
4. **Evaluation completeness: open outside scope.** Multi-seed LLM validation and an adaptive same-batch ESS theorem remain unavailable.
5. **Method soundness: pass with qualification.** The proof is valid without parameter projection under the stated pathwise smoothness and feasibility conditions. Active projection is correctly excluded from the Euclidean-gradient theorem.
