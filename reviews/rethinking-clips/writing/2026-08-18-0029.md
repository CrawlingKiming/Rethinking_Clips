# Theory-first framing and organization self-review

## Revised section outline

1. Introduce the empirical contradiction: clipping is treated as necessary, yet permissive updates can learn faster before collapsing.
2. Identify the missing explanation as effective support, measured by sequence ESS.
3. Build the theory from rollout coverage to gradient MSE, policy improvement and convergence, and the clipping bias--variance crossover.
4. Validate those theoretical links with an exactly evaluable contextual-bandit policy-optimization experiment.
5. Use a fixed ESS-conditioned intervention only as an operational test in language-model RLVR.
6. Put the compact testbed definition, oracle, pseudocode, diagnostics, and supporting tables in Appendix A.

## Five-dimension skeptical review

1. **Contribution: pass.** The paper now leads with the missing theoretical link and treats the conditional update only as a testable implication.
2. **Writing clarity: pass.** The abstract and introduction use high-level language first, and notation begins only in the preliminaries.
3. **Experimental strength: pass in scope.** The controlled experiment is organized around three theory predictions before reporting the heuristic intervention.
4. **Evaluation completeness: qualified.** LLM plots are trajectory-level evidence because multi-seed uncertainty is unavailable, and the manuscript says so.
5. **Method soundness: pass with qualification.** ESS is not equated with full gradient MSE; gradient scale, clipping bias, and construction-specific crossover behavior remain explicit.

## Reviewer-facing conclusion

**Verdict: PASS IN SCOPE.** The paper now reads as an explanation of when clipping helps, not as a proposal for a new ESS optimizer. The controlled and LLM experiments are downstream tests of that explanation. Appendix A is substantially shorter while retaining the quantities required to understand the oracle gradient, MSE, crossover diagnostic, and optimization protocol.
