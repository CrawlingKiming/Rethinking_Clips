## Claim under review

The paper argues that sequence ESS diagnoses likelihood-ratio concentration in reused RLVR batches, while the decision to clip must compare covariance reduction against squared gradient bias. It derives this comparison for a fixed full-sequence estimator, connects gradient MSE to a smooth stationarity bound, and proposes empirical tests for an ESS-indexed crossover.

## Competing hypotheses

1. **Estimator-crossover hypothesis.** Low sequence ESS increases the raw full-sequence gradient MSE enough that a bounded estimator becomes preferable despite its bias.
2. **Gradient-scale hypothesis.** Apparent failures associated with low ESS are driven mainly by large or poorly aligned score contributions, so ESS adds little once weighted gradient scale is observed.
3. **Optimizer-dynamics hypothesis.** Adam state, learning rate, and policy-dependent data collection determine the practical reversal; pointwise estimator MSE is not the main mechanism.
4. **Surrogate-mismatch hypothesis.** The effect is specific to token-local PPO/GRPO/CISPO objectives and cannot be predicted from the exact full-sequence change-of-measure estimator.
5. **Diagnostic-artifact hypothesis.** Sample ESS appears predictive because the same finite batch selects the regime and supplies the gradient; split-batch or cross-fitted gating removes or changes the effect.

The direct-resampling, matched-state, and split-batch experiments in the revised draft distinguish these explanations. None has yet been run.

## Round 1: adversarial critiques

1. **The practical method is incomplete.** The aggressive loss, conservative loss, detach convention, coefficient range, and threshold-calibration rule are unspecified.
2. **There is no empirical evidence.** The central single-crossing hypothesis, practical gate, and any numerical threshold remain untested.
3. **The novelty boundary is narrow.** P3O, VCPO, M2PO, and VESPO already adapt off-policy LLM updates using ESS, second moments, or sequence weights.
4. **The fixed-learner theorem does not cover adaptive same-batch reuse.** In the inner loop, the learner depends on the batch used to estimate its gradient, so the pointwise unbiasedness statement cannot be applied conditionally without further stability or uniform-convergence control.
5. **The oracle gate is not the implemented gate.** Population ESS and true MSE ordering are unavailable, and same-batch sample ESS creates selection dependence.
6. **The exact estimator is not the practical objective.** Full-sequence importance weighting for a terminal reward is algebraically distinct from token-local PPO, GRPO, and CISPO gradients.
7. **Some bounds rely on restrictive contribution control.** The transparent ESS and cap envelopes use an almost-sure bound on the sequence score contribution, which can be unrealistic for neural policies.
8. **The clipped update was initially ambiguous.** If the clipping coefficient is differentiated rather than detached, the chain rule changes the estimator and invalidates the stated bias formula.

8. A new remark defines the modified coefficient as detached and warns that differentiating a clipped scalar objective introduces additional chain-rule terms.

## Surviving critiques

### Critical

1. **Missing method specification and results.** The practical algorithm cannot be reproduced or evaluated until the objective slots are filled, and no performance or mechanism claim is supported yet. **Fix:** specify both losses and calibration rule before training; then complete Experiments A--D with uncertainty estimates.

2. **Theory-to-practice bridge is unvalidated.** The theorem concerns a fixed full-sequence estimator, whereas the intended use concerns adaptive, token-local objectives. **Fix:** prioritize direct resampling and matched-state forks before presenting the gate as a method.

### Major

3. **Novelty depends on the empirical finding.** Existing adaptive off-policy LLM methods already exploit ESS and second moments. **Fix:** compare directly against P3O, VCPO, M2PO, and VESPO and show whether measuring the MSE crossover changes decisions or outcomes beyond their controls.

4. **Adaptive same-batch dependence remains outside the theory.** **Fix:** include split-batch/cross-fitted diagnostics and report whether the crossover survives; a later theoretical extension could use stability or uniform concentration.

5. **Sample-gate error is unmeasured.** **Fix:** estimate regime-classification error relative to resampled MSE labels and report the excess-risk term suggested by the independent-pilot proposition.

### Minor

6. **The contribution may look algebraically straightforward.** **Fix:** keep the exactness claim modest and let a robust, transferable empirical crossover carry the significance.

## Dismissed

- The bounded-contribution assumption does not undermine the central exact MSE comparison; it affects only simplified envelopes.
- The detached-coefficient ambiguity has been resolved explicitly.

## Mathematical audit summary

- The centered policy-improvement identity is valid when target and behavior policies share the prompt distribution, $P_\theta\ll Q$, and the prompt-only baseline is detached.
- The raw gradient MSE identity is the standard covariance-trace identity for iid samples and is correct under the stated finite joint second moment.
- The clipping crossover follows exactly from bias--variance decomposition.
- The upper-cap bias and variance envelopes are correct under the stated almost-sure bound on $H_\theta$.
- The smooth ascent theorem follows from the descent/ascent lemma and the polarization identity for any possibly biased update with $\eta\le 1/L$.
- The independent-pilot gate proposition follows by conditioning on the selected estimator; it deliberately does not apply to an unsplit same-batch gate.
